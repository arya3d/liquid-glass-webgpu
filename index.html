Field notes on light

Glass bends
what it shows

Why a straw looks broken

Light slows down when it enters water, and the change of speed bends its path. Your eye assumes light travels straight, so it places the straw where it is not.

Thick glass bends more

A lens is thickest in the middle. Light crossing the centre is pulled further than light near the rim, which is why the world behind it swells.

Colours split at the edge

Blue light bends a little more than red. Where the glass is steepest the colours land in slightly different places, and a thin rainbow fringes every edge.

Edges catch the light

At a grazing angle glass reflects more than it lets through. That is why the rim of a glass shines brighter than its face.
